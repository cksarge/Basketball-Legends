# Changelog

Every release of Basketball Legends is listed here, newest first. Each version's section doubles as its release notes on GitHub.

## v0.3.0 (in beta)

In testing at legends.carterscoding.com/beta/. Career mode is here: one player, from a freshman tryout to the Hall of Fame and a shot at becoming a Basketball Legend.

### Road to Legend

Career mode has a new name and one ultimate goal: become a Basketball Legend.

- **Legend ranks:** Local Hero, State Star, National Name, Pro, All-Star, Superstar, Icon and Legend. The Road to Legend card in the career hub shows your rank, your points, what's next and your latest milestones.
- **Four ways to earn it:** Greatness (honors), Winning (titles and tournament runs), Moments (big games you played yourself, never simulated ones) and Impact (community work and the classroom). Careers already under way get credit for what they've done.
- **Legend is very hard:** the title is only decided at retirement, and only for Icons. Once you're an Icon, the game shows your chance of becoming a Legend.

### Create your player

- **Start where you want:** high school (the full road), college at the program of your choice, or the pros, through the draft or straight onto a team. Starting later means less time to build a legacy.
- **Play style badges:** pick up to two, like Sharpshooter or Lockdown. Each lifts two skills and how high they can grow.
- **Assign me a school:** let the game pick from your hometown's schools.
- **A Legend's kid:** a Legend can pass it on. Start their child's career with the family name, a head start, and your Legend as your mentor.

### Your career hub

- **Calendar:** a month view of your games, results and the dates that matter, next to the schedule list.
- **Stat history:** charts of your points, rebounds, assists, minutes and overall by season, and your scoring game by game.
- **Goals and milestones:** what to chase at every stage, checked off from what you've done.
- **Sim to next event:** sims until something needs you, at every stage.
- **Your scrapbook:** everything that has built your legacy, filtered by Greatness, Winning, Moments or Impact.

### Your story

- **Press conferences and interviews:** media day every season, postgame interviews after big nights and tough losses, plus the draft, trades, the trade deadline and championships. Answer, or walk past the microphones.
- **Personality and reputation:** every answer moves you between Humble and Confident, and between Team-first and Star, and changes how fans, the media, teammates and coaches see you.
- **It matters:** coaches and teammates affect your trust, minutes and chemistry, fans bring bigger endorsements, and the media moves your draft stock.
- **Your phone:** a new Phone tab with texts from your family and a mentor from the Hall of Legends, who has followed you since freshman year.
- **Social feed:** fans and reporters react to your big nights, awards, trades and injuries.
- **A rival:** a player from a nearby school follows you to college and the pros with a Legend score of their own. Rival games are flagged, and the Legends tab tracks your head-to-head record.
- **Commitment ceremony:** hats on the table in front of the school when you commit.
- **Decisions:** dozens of high school, college and pro events, from parties and booster offers to locker room fights and a foundation back home.
- **Impact:** community choices now earn Impact points on the Road to Legend, including in the pros.
- **Story setting:** the how-often setting from the creator now works.

### The park in your career

- **Park tab:** three pickup runs every off-season, starting the summer before freshman year. Play them in 3D, or sim them for half the reputation.
- **Street reputation:** runs earn reputation and XP, and tougher parks pay more. Your name opens new cities, from Phoenix and your neighborhood court to the Boardwalk, the Cage and the rooftop.
- **Drop-ins:** known pros sometimes show up for a run. Beat one in a game you play and it counts as a Legend Moment.
- **Call-outs:** your rival calls you out some summers for one-on-one to 11. Winning counts on your head-to-head, and ducking it costs you.
- **Regulars:** the same faces are there every time, and the Park tab keeps your record against them.
- **Legend rewards:** a Basketball Legend's name goes on the hometown courts. At their peak ratings, they can join any team in Quick Play and be picked in standalone Play in the Park. Retired players can keep playing runs.

### College

- **Move in on campus:** high school graduates start college at the program they picked, on scholarship or as a walk-on.
- **Coach trust and minutes:** your overall and your coach's trust set your role (starter, rotation or end of the bench), and the coach's lineup and minutes follow it in real games. Beat what the coach expects and your role grows. Struggle and it shrinks. Walk-ons start with less trust and can earn a scholarship.
- **Real seasons in all three divisions:** every game in your division is simulated, with non-conference games, conference play, conference tournaments and national rankings. Your own games run through the real game engine in two halves.
- **The postseason:** conference tournament winners earn automatic bids to a 68-team National Tournament with an opening round, and a 32-team National Invitational takes the next best in Division I. Divisions II and III have 64-team tournaments.
- **Honors:** All-Conference, Freshman of the Year, conference Player of the Year, All-American, National Player of the Year and Most Outstanding Player.
- **NIL:** local, community and national deals every season. Community deals pay less but count toward your legacy, and big national deals cost a little coach trust.
- **After each season:** come back, enter the transfer portal or declare for the draft with your projected draft stock. Withdrawing from the portal costs some trust. You get four years of eligibility and one redshirt.

### The draft

- **Declaring starts the road to the draft:** pick from three agents (bigger contracts, better endorsements or a smaller fee), then go through the combine, three team workouts and GM interviews. Each step moves your draft stock, and your interview answers shape your personality.
- **The combine:** shooting, agility and vertical drills with real measurements.
- **Draft night:** a live broadcast from the arena, with the team on the clock, a running board and your player on the big screen when your name is called. Two rounds, 60 picks, a lottery at the top and trades as they happen. Skip to your pick or watch it all.
- **Rookie deals:** a salary scale through the first round and small two-year deals in the second.
- **Undrafted:** fringe prospects get two-way offers, and anyone can join a development team.

### The pros

- **Full 82-game seasons:** every game in the league is simulated, with a real schedule mix of division, conference and interconference games, live standings and your own games through the real game engine.
- **Coach, role and chemistry:** your overall and coach trust set your role (starter, sixth man, rotation or end of the bench) and your real minutes. Team chemistry moves with wins and losses and adds to your team's strength.
- **Playoffs:** a six-team play-in, then four best-of-seven rounds through the Finals.
- **All-Star weekend:** the best players are voted in for a playable East vs West game. Top shooters enter a 3-point contest you can play, and the best leapers go for the dunk contest.
- **Honors:** MVP, All-League teams, Defensive Player of the Year, Rookie of the Year, Sixth Man, Most Improved, Finals MVP and more.
- **Contracts:** when your deal ends you hit free agency with offers from your team and others. Ask for more and they may agree or walk away. Teams can promise a role, and a broken promise makes a trade yours for the asking.
- **Trades:** ask for one before the deadline, at a cost with your coach and teammates.
- **Injuries:** injuries from games keep you out while your team plays on.
- **Training and aging:** pick a focus at training camp every summer, with optional practice drills that make the work go further. After 30 you lose a step, and the legs go first.
- **Badges:** great seasons earn badges like Deadeye and Walking Bucket that lift a rating for good.
- **Endorsements:** fame brings brand deals, and a well-connected agent gets you more.
- **Development league and two-way deals:** undrafted players play a 30-game season and can earn a call-up. Two-way players split the year, with games on the league roster too.

### The end of the road

- **Legend ranks in the pros:** All-Star, Superstar and Icon are now reachable, and pro honors, playoff wins, scoring milestones and big games you play yourself all count.
- **Your odds:** once you're an Icon, the Road to Legend card shows your chance of becoming a Legend at retirement.
- **Playoff intensity:** opponents raise their level in the playoffs, most of all in the conference finals and the Finals.
- **League's best:** awards now go to whoever beats that year's best rivals, so a great year can still come up short and an MVP is never automatic.
- **Hall of Legends:** a new Legends tab ranks you against the all-time greats in six records, and lists everything that has built your legacy.
- **Retirement:** retire from age 30 on, or when the offers stop. Your jersey is retired where you earned it, the Hall of Fame votes, you give a speech shaped by who you thank first, and the final verdict comes in.
- **Basketball Legend:** the title goes only to an Icon who retires with 2,400 Legend points.

### Play in the Park

- **Earn your parks:** Copper Flats and Maple Street are open to everyone. The Boardwalk, the Ironside Cage and the rooftop open in standalone Play in the Park once any of your careers earns the street reputation for them.
- **Call your own fouls** is now a choice: turn it off and the game plays straight through.
- **Local players:** extra controllers can join either side in team games.
- **Your players:** pick any of your career players, along with regulars and league players.

### Phones and tablets

- **Controller check:** the game has no touch controls, so games on a touch-only device now ask you to connect a controller, and that controller takes over when it connects. A physical keyboard works too. Menus, career management and simming still work by touch.

### Menu and How to Play

- **A trailer on the main menu:** the arena flyover is now a cinematic trailer built from the game itself, opening on a fast break and running through a slow-motion dunk, a postgame interview, the Boardwalk at sunset and a half-court buzzer-beater before ending on a lone Legend. It plays the same way every time, and holds on a still image with Reduce motion on.
- **New main menu order:** Resume, Quick Play, Road to Legend, Franchise, Multiplayer, Play in the Park, Practice and Tutorial, Settings, How to play.
- **Quick Play has a fast start:** Play now is gone from the menu. Fast start in Quick Play jumps into a game with two random teams.
- **Practice and Tutorial are one option,** with the tutorial a button on the practice screen.
- **How to Play is easier to read,** with short sections and lists in place of long paragraphs.

### Fixes

- **Smoother defaults:** auto-detect now picks High on Apple silicon Macs, which holds a steadier frame rate than Ultra. Ultra is still in Settings.
- **Graphics settings stick:** your graphics preset is no longer reset to the auto-detected one every time a page loads.
- **Changelog:** the top of this page now links to the beta from the main game, and back to the main game from the beta.
- **Main menu in the beta:** the beta warning and the Resume game panel no longer overlap. They stack in one column, and the panel scrolls in short windows.

### High school and recruiting

- **Create a player:** first and last name, Arizona hometown (114 cities) and high school, any of the 283 schools through open enrollment, and jersey number.
- **Position and archetype:** the archetype sets your starting ratings and how high each skill can grow.
- **Body:** pick the adult height you grow into by senior year, plus weight and wingspan. Every rating gets a cap from your body: taller and heavier players can grow size skills higher, smaller and lighter ones speed and handles, and a long wingspan helps on defense. Freshmen start shorter and grow through high school.
- **Look:** skin tone, hair, facial hair, face shape, shoes, headband, arm sleeve and knee pads, with a live 3D preview in your school's uniform.
- **Starting skills:** spend 45 points (up to 10 per skill) or auto-fill by archetype.
- **Career settings:** difficulty, quarter length, what happens when you go to the bench, and how often story events appear.
- **Several careers:** each saves in your browser after everything you do. Export a career as a `.blcareer` file to back it up or move it, and import it on any device.
- **High school seasons:**
  - **Full schedules for all 283 schools:** home-and-away region play from January (single round robin in regions of eight or more), non-region games from Thanksgiving week, and a holiday tournament between Christmas and New Year's.
  - **Tryouts:** three drills in November (spot shooting, defensive slides, conditioning) whose results nudge where the coach places you. A great freshman can make varsity; most start on the freshman/sophomore team or JV. Seniors always make varsity, and schools with only a varsity team start you there.
  - **Call-ups and send-downs:** after each game the coach looks at your last three games and your overall rating, and moves you up a level or back down.
  - **Your games:** play each one in 3D with control locked to your player, or sim one, three, or the rest of the season. Simulated games still run through the real game engine, so your box scores are authentic.
  - **The bench:** when the coach sits you in a game you're playing, choose to watch, fast-forward until you check back in, or sim to the end, and optionally remember the choice.
  - **State playoffs:** an Open Division for the 16 best 6A–4A teams, 16-team brackets for 6A–3A and 24-team brackets with a play-in round for 2A and 1A, seeded by power rankings (win percentage, strength of schedule and margin). The state championship is at a neutral site.
  - **Every school's season is simulated:** standings, region tables, and a state champion in every class.
  - **Development:** ratings grow on their own every few weeks toward the caps your body allows, faster when you're young, with more room to grow, or with a strong work ethic. XP from games and practice earns skill points to spend on any rating, costing more near the cap. You also grow taller through high school.
  - **Teammates who stay:** your school's rosters carry over year to year. Everyone moves up a class and improves, seniors graduate, the best underclassmen move up a level, and a new freshman class arrives.
  - **Honors:** region champion, All-Region first and second teams, Region Player of the Year, All-State teams, Player of the Year and state champion, all in your career log.
  - **School:** pick your classroom aptitude in the creator, then split your time between basketball and school with a slider (more basketball means more practice XP, more school means better grades). Fall and spring finals are a quick eight-question exam against the clock. A fall GPA under 2.0 makes you academically ineligible for the rest of the season, and your games are simulated without you.
  - **Summers:** choose a June camp (shooting, ball-handling, big man, defense, or speed and strength, plus the invite-only Elite 100 for top-300 prospects) for rating boosts and exposure, then play three July AAU showcase games, on the elite circuit if you're ranked. Play them in 3D or sim them.
  - **Recruiting rankings:** scouts rate your class on your projected senior-year overall, your varsity production and your exposure from camps and showcases. You get a star rating, a national rank and an Arizona rank that update after every season and summer event, with a message when your stars change.
  - **Career hub:** a next-up card, messages from your coach and trainer, recent games, a full schedule with box scores, season and career averages, rosters for every level, the region table, the state bracket, and ratings with skill-point upgrades.
  - **College recruiting:** from June 15 after sophomore year, programs across D1, D2 and D3 start following you. Interest depends on your recruiting score compared with the level each program usually signs (blue bloods land top-40 prospects, mid-majors three-stars, small colleges unranked players), with a home-state edge for Arizona schools. Division I needs a 2.3 core GPA: let your grades slip and D1 offers are pulled, then come back when your grades recover. Up to 30 offers, each with the coaches' pitch for your freshman role and an NIL estimate.
  - **Recruiting tab:** every program on your board with its interest in you and yours in it, filters for offers and your shortlist, five official visits (sit down with the coach, hang out with the players, tour the facilities or meet an academic advisor, each with its own story and effect), commit and decommit, and letters of intent signed on the early (November) or late (April) signing day. A signed letter is binding.
  - **Reclassify:** four- and five-star juniors with a 3.0 GPA can graduate a year early and skip senior year.
  - **Graduation:** pick your college first, signing with an offer or walking on at a program that would take you.
  - **Balance:** skill points cost more XP (60 instead of 40), so a great player finishes high school as a top recruit without being better than most college players.
- **Career save slots:** Career now has three save slots of its own, separate from the three shared by Quick Play and the park, so a career game can sit next to other games without overwriting them. Choose Save game in the pause menu, and the career hub resumes the game from any slot or the autosave. Resume game on the main menu lists the Career slots too. A saved career game is cleared once you sim it or delete that career.
- **Real high school levels:** each Arizona school now knows which boys basketball teams it fields (varsity, JV, JV 2, freshman/sophomore), from the AIA's official AZPreps365 team pages. 205 of the 283 schools run a freshman/sophomore team.

## v0.2.0 — October 28, 2026

Play in the Park: pickup basketball on outdoor courts, from the main menu.

### Parks

- **Five original courts:** Copper Flats Courts in Phoenix (a desert park with a shade ramada, saguaros and mountains), Ironside Cage in New York (a fenced court between brick buildings at sunset), Boardwalk Courts in Los Angeles (a beach court with palms and the ocean at sunset), Maple Street Park in Chicago (a neighborhood blacktop under the lights) and Rooftop on Ninth in Kansas City (a court on a parking garage with the skyline lit up all around).
- **Outdoor courts:** worn asphalt with cracks and patches, or painted sport surfaces; chain-link fences; light poles that come on at night; poles with steel or acrylic backboards and chain or nylon nets; daylight, sunset and night skies with shadows to match.
- **Park regulars:** each park has 18 regulars with nicknames, reputations ("Never misses from the corner", "Calls next every game") and street clothes, the same people every time you show up. Some parks are friendly runs, others are high-level. Older regulars have lost a step but know every trick.
- **Anyone can play:** pick your player and every spot from the park's regulars or from any team in the three leagues, or shuffle the regulars.

### Pickup rules

- **1-on-1 up to 5-on-5**, on a half court or a full court.
- **Scoring:** 1s and 2s or 2s and 3s, games to 11, 15 or 21, win by 2 (or not).
- **Make it, take it** or losers' ball after every basket.
- **Check ball:** half court, the ball is checked at the top of the key after every basket, foul and turnover.
- **Take it back:** half court, after a miss, a steal or a rebound, the ball has to go back past the arc before you can score. A basket that wasn't taken back doesn't count.
- **Call your own fouls:** no referees and no free throws. The fouled player checks it back up, a charge goes the other way, and nobody fouls out.
- **No clock:** no game clock, shot clock or timeouts. The AI still moves the ball and looks for its shot.
- **Street sounds:** no whistles, buzzers or announcer, and a few onlookers instead of an arena crowd.

### More ways to play

- **Next game up:** your crew calls next against the crew holding the court, with up to four crews waiting. Winners stay on, losers go to the back of the line, and the games ahead of you are played out while you wait. The line tracks your winning streak and every result.
- **21:** three to six players, each on their own: whoever has the ball plays against everyone else. Score and you shoot free throws until you miss (make three and you check it again). Hit the target exactly: go over and you drop back to 13.
- **HORSE:** against up to three regulars. Make a shot and everyone has to match it from the same spot, or take a letter. Play HORSE or PIG.
- **3-point contest:** 25 balls on five racks in 60 seconds, with a gold money ball worth 2 on every rack, against up to five rivals.

## v0.1.1 — October 28, 2026

A patch for shooting and the main menu.

### Shooting

- **Perfect releases go in:** a green release now goes in about 97% of the time for any shooter, and still about 85% with a hand in your face. Before, the meter's green band and the game's green window didn't match, so many releases that showed "Perfect release" were scored as merely good. Even a true green on a contested three only went in about half the time.
- **Fairer green windows:** the window is wider (about 15 ms either side of perfect for poor shooters, 22 ms for average and 40 ms for great ones), and the green band on the meter is drawn at exactly that size, so a better shooter visibly gets a bigger target.
- **Smoother meter:** the bar now updates every frame while you hold the shot instead of 20 times a second, so where you see it is where it really is.

### Main menu

- **Same spacing on every screen:** the menu no longer squeezes together in short windows; if it doesn't fit, it scrolls, and the arena backdrop stays fixed behind it.
- **Only the menu scrolls on wide screens:** the title, the info card and the footer links stay in place while the menu column scrolls, with soft fades at its top and bottom edges.

## v0.1.0 — October 28, 2026

The first public version: a full 5v5 basketball game in the browser. You can play any pro, college or Arizona high school team in Quick Play, practice, or learn the controls in the tutorial. Career, Franchise and online play come in later versions.

### Leagues and teams

- **Three leagues, 1,405 teams:** the Pro Basketball League (30 cities), National Collegiate Hoops (1,092 programs in D-I, D-II and D-III across 100 conferences), and Arizona Prep Hoops (283 high schools, 6A through 1A, using the 2026–28 region alignment).
- Real city, school and university names identify places. Every nickname, logo, color scheme, conference, arena and player is original and fictional.
- The Brophy Prep Broncos appear in their real red and white with the school's official logo on the court, the jerseys and the menus.
- Full generated rosters with ratings, positions, heights and bench depth for every team.

### Gameplay

- **Real-time 3D 5v5** with dribble moves, a timing-based shot meter, layups, dunks, floaters, hooks, stepback fadeaways, post moves, pump fakes, alley-oops, steals, blocks and box-out rebounding.
- **Full rules for each league:** shot clocks, personal and hand-check fouls, the bonus, free throws, inbounds, traveling, three seconds, backcourt and 8/10-second violations, goaltending, timeouts, substitutions, fatigue and overtime.
- **Team AI** that spaces the floor, runs pick-and-roll, drives and kicks, posts up and cuts, and plays man or zone defense with help rotations.
- **Coaching tactics:** pace, three-point emphasis, offensive rebounding, double teams, and man-to-man, 2-3 zone, 3-2 zone or a full-court trapping press. Set them before the game or from the pause menu.
- **Gameplay sliders:** shot, three-point and layup success, pass accuracy, steals, blocks, fouls, game speed, injuries and CPU aggression.
- **Injuries (optional):** falls, hard drives and awkward landings can hurt players, with forced substitutions and estimates of games missed.
- Stat tuning per league so pro, college and high school box scores look like the real thing.

### Modes

- **Quick Play:** any two teams from any league (cross-league exhibitions included), instant simulation, and up to four local players on keyboard and gamepads.
- **Tutorial:** a ten-step guided session (moving, sprinting, dribble moves, shot timing, layups, threes, passing, pump fakes, replays) that checks off each skill as you do it, using your own bindings, and keeps your progress through pauses.
- **Practice:** a shootaround with no clock and no defense, with live shooting stats.
- **Resume game:** games save automatically every few seconds. Close the tab or choose Save and quit, then pick Resume game on the main menu to continue exactly where you left off.

### Venues

- **Seven venues built in Blender:** three high school gyms (small, classic and modern, with wooden or plastic bleachers, girders or open-web joists, and a stage with curtains in the oldest ones), a college fieldhouse with an arched brick-and-steel roof, a two-deck campus arena, and two pro arenas with a suites ring, a club level and a halo video board.
- Each team's home is picked from its capacity and dressed in its colors: seats in team sections, painted walls and pads, LED ribbons, wordmarks, wall logos, live scoreboards, and championship and retired-number banners that match the program's history.
- Solid hoop bases, and rims that shake on hard hits by hinging at the backboard.

### Presentation

- Broadcast HUD, six cameras (including a courtside low camera), and commentary with optional voice.
- **Instant replays and highlights:** replay any moment with scrubbing, slow motion and five replay cameras. Big plays replay automatically (setting: after baskets, all big plays, or off), and a highlight reel is ready at every break and after the game.
- **Officials:** a three-person crew works every game. They toss the opening jump ball, take lead, trail and center positions that rotate on every change of possession, blow whistles with real signals, show the five-second count, and hand the ball to inbounders and free-throw shooters. The scorer's table crew sits courtside.
- **Game flow:** quarter, halftime and timeout screens with line scores, team stats and leaders; team huddles; benches full of players who react to big plays; name tags; big-play banners (AND ONE, REJECTED, runs, buzzer beaters); broadcast stat cards for hot scorers and free-throw shooters.
- **Crowd:** a synthesized home crowd with arena reverb that murmurs, roars, applauds, oohs, awws, boos and chants DE-FENSE, reacts differently to home and away plays, and jumps up with arms raised on big plays.
- **Sound:** every sound is generated by the game, including footsteps on real foot contacts, sneaker squeaks on hard cuts, and one dribble sound per bounce.
- **Animation:** running with knee drive and arm pump, defensive slides that never cross the feet, natural falls, winded hands-on-knees, catches, steal lunges, chinned post-ups, and one-handed dribbling on the correct side.
- Cameras never clip through a venue's walls, seats or roof.

### Menus, settings and accessibility

- Main menu with keyboard and gamepad navigation; menu games use the last-used controller.
- Loading screens with real progress and gameplay tips that use your own key bindings.
- Graphics presets with auto-detect, full key and button remapping, colorblind modes, subtitles, reduced motion, UI scale and tap-to-shoot.
- How to Play, Credits, Terms of Service, Privacy Policy and this changelog. The version number in the main menu footer links here.

### Works anywhere

- Runs in any modern browser with no download or account.
- **Offline play:** after one visit the whole game is cached and playable without a connection, and it can be installed as an app.
- Settings and saved games stay in your browser. No accounts, ads, analytics or cookies.
- **The beta:** legends.carterscoding.com/beta/ runs the next version while it's being tested. A card on its main menu explains that it shares saves with the main game and links back to the main game. It has its own offline copy and is kept out of search results.

### Under the hood

- Static Next.js site on GitHub Pages at legends.carterscoding.com, open source under the MIT License. The `main` branch is the live game and the `prerelease` branch is the beta; one workflow builds, tests and publishes both.
- Every deploy typechecks, lints, checks formatting with Prettier, and runs the unit tests (rules, injuries, saves, input, deterministic save and resume), a camera sweep that flies every camera through every venue and fails if any clip, and browser smoke tests for every page.
